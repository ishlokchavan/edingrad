# Edingrad — Brand Voice (The Edingrad Standard)

> Source of truth for all copy in this repo — UI microcopy, page content, emails,
> PDF reports, error messages. Derived from the Edingrad brand-guidelines Voice
> section (`edingrad-brand-guidelines`, `src/data/principles.ts` +
> `src/components/sections/Voice.tsx`). If anything here conflicts with the
> guidelines repo, the guidelines repo wins.

The Edingrad Standard governs how we write: **a calm, senior register that earns
trust through precision and restraint — never volume.** The voice stays constant;
only the *tone* moves with the reader.

The litmus test for everything: **"Would this read as Edingrad with the name
covered?"**

---

## Three pillars (the voice)

| Pillar | Essence | Directive |
|---|---|---|
| **Analytical Rigor** | Evidence before assertion. | Lead with the data and the reasoning. Every claim is traceable to a source or a method; nothing is asserted that cannot be defended. |
| **Discerning Intuition** | Judgement, earned. | Pattern recognition built on experience. We name what the numbers imply but cannot say outright — carefully, and never as a substitute for the work. |
| **Measured Gravitas** | Weight without noise. | A calm, senior register. We are never breathless, never promotional. Confidence is conveyed through precision and restraint. |

---

## Four principles (how we write)

Use them as guidance, not gospel.

### 1. Be precise
Precision is the whole point. One right word beats three approximate ones; a
qualifier that carries no weight is just noise.
- **Cut the qualifiers** — delete "potentially", "in order to", and every hedge that softens a claim without sharpening it.
- **Name the specific** — "Reduce the position by a third", not "trim exposure".
- **One idea per sentence** — each should carry a single claim the reader can weigh.

> ✗ We aim to potentially help optimise risk-adjusted outcomes.
> ✓ We allocate where capital compounds.

### 2. Lead with the finding
State the conclusion first, then show the work. A reader should know where they
stand by the end of the first sentence.
- **Conclusion first** — open with the decision or the view; context and caveats come after.
- **Show the method** — make every claim traceable to a source or a model.
- **State the confidence** — name the conviction level and the caveats, so the reader can size the risk.

> ✗ After extensive multi-scenario analysis, a view was formed on the position.
> ✓ Reduce the position. The downside is no longer priced in.

### 3. Write to one reader
Address a single, intelligent person — never a market. Formality is a barrier;
clarity is courtesy.
- **One to one** — write "you", to one decision-maker. Drop the committee voice and the passive "it is recommended".
- **Translate to consequence** — don't stop at what is true; say what it means for the reader's next move.
- **Read it aloud** — if you wouldn't say it across a table to them, rewrite it.

> ✗ Stakeholders should be cognisant of the multifaceted risk factors herein.
> ✓ Here is the one risk that should change your mind — and why.

### 4. Senior, never loud
Authority is shown through restraint, not volume. We never sell, oversell, or shout.
- **Refuse hype** — no "explosive", "once-in-a-generation", "guaranteed".
- **Let restraint carry** — understate; a measured line outlasts a loud one.
- **Take the long view** — favour what compounds over what flatters today.

> ✗ A once-in-a-generation, can't-miss opportunity with explosive upside.
> ✓ The asymmetry favours the patient holder. We are buyers.

## Vocabulary

**Prefer:** thesis · conviction · diligence · allocation · downside · mandate · measured · evidence

**Avoid (hype):** guaranteed · unprecedented · game-changing · hot · explosive · no-brainer · sure thing · hype

---

## Tone spectrum

The voice is constant; the tone moves along these axes by context.

| Axis | ← | → | Guidance |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Register** | Conversational | Formal | Lean formal for institutional memos; relax toward conversational for client letters and the site. |
| **Pace** | Brisk | Considered | Brisk for alerts and decisions; considered for thesis pieces that earn their length. |
| **Warmth** | Reserved | Warm | Reserved with peers who want the signal; warmer with private clients who want reassurance. |

---

## Checklist before publishing

- Would this read as Edingrad with the name covered?
- Can a non-specialist follow the argument?
- Does every figure earn its place?
- Is the most important thing the most prominent thing?
- Have we said it plainly, without hedging or hype?
- Is there a single word of hype we would regret in a downturn?
